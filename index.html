Chart detailsPress and hold a chart for its detail window. Pick a range inside it. Tap outside to close.
Station windowOne button on each page opens one window with five tabs, in this order: Forecasts, Best source, Rain test, Records and Health. Tap a tab to switch without closing. On Station Forecast it opens Forecasts; on Station History it opens Health. The button’s dot shows the last check. A red dot on the left (and on the Records tab) means the battery is low.
Health tabIts small dot shows the last check: green working, amber worth a look, red can’t reach the Mac mini or no recent reading. Connections: red means something can’t be reached; amber means versions differ, so close and reopen the app. The Tempest and Environment Canada forecast lights live here too, with Check forecasts now. Data checks never turn the result red: amber means repeat readings (ignored in every total) or today’s rain differs from Tempest online.
1Open-Meteo GEM
Best source tabRanks Open-Meteo GEM, Environment Canada and Tempest online hour by hour: every finished hour is checked against each one’s forecast from 1 up to 24 hours before, against what your station measured. Temperature and Feels Like sit in two columns, best first, all time, with a day-by-day table below. Tap a source for why it ranks there (including how it does near term versus a day out). Its lights show whether each source is saving.
Rain test tabWith today’s rain hour by hour against Tempest online. Pick a start time and empty your manual gauge then; Station and Tempest online both count from 0. To finish, enter the gauge’s rain and tap Stop: it shows how far each is from the gauge. A yellow dot on the Station button means a test is scheduled or running.

History test✓ means rain was called right. Press and hold the panel for the full breakdown and the comparison with Environment Canada.
Learning testForecasts tab, Learning test switch. The forecast learns from your station’s own history. This opens a test that replays that history and shows green or red whether learning beats the classic method. It only goes live when green.
Amankenzie vs Tempest vs Environment CanadaForecasts tab, Sources. Compares your Amankenzie Learned forecast (the learning method, in yellow, first), your classic Amankenzie forecast (coral), Tempest’s own cloud forecast and Environment Canada’s, all scored against what your station measured: temperature, rain, and feels-like. Tap any card in the Temperature or Feels-like section (View data) for every hour behind its score. They are saved every hour from the day it is switched on, so it fills in over weeks. Needs a one-time setup on the Mac mini; its saving lights are on the Health tab.
Classic and learning forecasts
9pm8°
ClassicUses your station’s last 9 hours of readings and a standard day and night curve. It does not learn from earlier days. Its hour cards have no star.
9pm8°
Learning, with a starA star in the top-left corner of an hour card means the learning forecast made it. It also learns from all your saved history: hours that looked like now, at the same time of day and time of year, and what the temperature did next. It corrects its rain chances against how often it really rained. It uses only your station and the clock.
When it switches onOnly when the learning test is green. Learning then takes over, and the star appears. If a later test turns red, the classic forecast returns and the stars disappear.
